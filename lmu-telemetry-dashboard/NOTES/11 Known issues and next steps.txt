11 KNOWN ISSUES AND NEXT STEPS
==============================

1. TIRE TEMPERATURES DON'T MATCH LMU  (open)
   The dashboard shows the tread SURFACE temperature. LMU may show a
   different reading (inner layer or carcass), or show Fahrenheit.
   To fix it, send:
     - a screenshot of http://localhost:3000/api/snapshot (the "tires" part)
       after 2-3 laps on track
     - the tire temperatures LMU shows at the same moment, and whether they
       are in C or F
   Each tire has three readings in the snapshot: "temps" (surface),
   "innerLayerTemps" and "carcassTempC". The one that matches LMU will
   become the dashboard's reading.
   Until then, the tire engineer's temperature advice isn't reliable.

2. WHEEL BUTTON WHILE LMU HAS FOCUS  (needs testing)
   Browsers may stop reading the wheel when another window is focused.
   Test: dashboard visible but NOT clicked, hold the wheel button, ask a
   question. Report whether it worked in Chrome and/or Edge.

3. LMU BUILT-IN TELEMETRY  (possible future change)
   Newer LMU versions have their own built-in telemetry output. If the rF2
   plugin ever stops working after an LMU update, the dashboard can be
   switched to LMU's built-in data (no plugin needed).

4. PIT LOSS AND WEAR LIMIT ARE ESTIMATES
   PIT_LOSS_SEC (35) and TIRE_WEAR_LIMIT_PERCENT (75) are guesses.
   Set them per track/car once you know the real values (see 06).

5. DAMAGE AND CRASHES
   The engineers now see body damage (8 zones), detached parts, flat tires,
   engine overheating and the last impact, and whether the car is on track,
   in the pit lane or in the garage. After a crash, ask "How bad is the
   damage?". If it still says something wrong, send a screenshot of
   http://localhost:3000/api/snapshot right after the crash ("damage" part).
