How to read it

Red means air is slowing down and pressing on the surface. Blue means it is speeding up and pulling, which is suction. A GT3 car makes downforce by keeping blue under the car and on the wing's underside, and red on the top surfaces.

F = ½ · ρ · v² · A · C

Every aero force grows with the square of speed. At 150 km/h you get only a quarter of the 300 km/h load. That is why fast corners feel planted and slow hairpins feel loose.

Add yaw and the car meets the air at an angle. The windward side builds pressure, the floor loses its seal, and the wing sits partly in the cabin's shadow. Downforce drops, drag climbs, and the balance moves forward. That forward shift is why a GT3 car can go loose on the exit of a long corner.

A teaching model. Forces come from a simplified coefficient model tuned to typical GT3 figures, and the flow field is an analytic approximation. It is not McLaren or CFD data.
